<h2>Own It</h2>
<p>The first book I read through was <em>Own it</em> by Michael and Hayley Dimarco. I loved it! The book was engaging from cover to cover. It laid out many of the common hindrances that we (as Christians) face when trying to truly take ownership of our faith, and embrace the precious gift of our salvation. These hindrances include faking, borrowing, picking and choosing and many more false facades we can portray with our faith. I tend to be a conscientious reader. All too often I find myself questioning the Biblical accuracy of books, but not this one. I was pleasantly surprised by its authentic truths. I even learned about personal struggles with owning my faith. I also appreciated that the authors included personal testimonies of their journey throughout the book. This authenticity helped me connect with the writers, and be open to accepting their words. The only thing I&#8217;d critique are some quotes that appeared in the book that were subjective, but stated more as fact than opinion; with bold implications. I thought they should have been either omitted or expounded upon.</p>
<h2>Grace Unplugged</h2>
<p>I must admit, for whatever reason, I have a hard time enjoying fiction. So please take that into consideration when reading my review.</p>
<p><em>Grace Unplugged </em>was a quick read with a good message of reconciliation, and the journey one takes to discover God. Unfortunately like many of us, the central character had to learn the hard way that God’s way is the best way. Yet even when we try to run away from God He will be ready with open arms to forgive us when we turn to Him. Although there was a good core message, the story line was predictable. It lacked twists and turns to truly captivate me. On occasion I felt as though I was reading the same dialogue I had just read in the previous chapter. Given the book&#8217;s length (approximately 300 pages) there was wiggle room to omit some of the repetition.</p>
