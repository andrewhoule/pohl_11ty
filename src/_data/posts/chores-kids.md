<p class="intro">Hello everyone, I had to share my new victory: chores for kids that they actually enjoy doing. It&#8217;s a magnetic chore chart, that they call a &#8220;game&#8221;, and voluntarily ask for&#8230; No, really! I&#8217;ve seen this concept on other websites; my biggest inspiration was from a website called <a href="http://aspottedpony.com/diy/make-cleaning-fun-for-kids-with-a-simple-diy-chore-chart/3867/">A Spotted Pony</a>. I gathered what worked for our family, omitted what didn&#8217;t and added my own twist on the subject. I&#8217;m a huge proponent of anything that keeps my sanity in tact. As a mother of 3, ages 4 and under, this is not always an easy task. I am not obsessed with cleaning and would rather spend time with people than clean any day of the week; however, it is important to my husband, so I have tried to make it a priority for myself as well. In the process I have started appreciating the journey.</p>
<p> Here is the finished project of my chore chart for my daughter, Izzy.[/caption]</p>
<h2>Here are some reasons why I think it&#8217;s great for kids to help out with chores</h2>
<ol>
<li>Many hands make lighter work (you&#8217;ll notice I didn&#8217;t say light work).</li>
<li>Parents get more time with kids.</li>
<li>Teach responsibility &#8211; If you mess it up, you should clean it.</li>
<li>Parents are less stressed &#8211; It keeps the kids busy cleaning instead of making another mess.</li>
</ol>
<h2>How to Make a Magnetic Chore Chart</h2>
<h3>Supplies Needed</h3>
<ul>
<li>Magnetic Roll, or Magnetic Sheet (I purchased mine at a hardware store)</li>
<li>Packaging Tape</li>
<li>Cookie/Pizza Sheet (bought at Dollar Tree)</li>
<li>Acrylic Paint (bought at Walmart .50-1.00 per bottle)</li>
<li>Paintbrush</li>
<li>Newspaper</li>
<li>Stickers/Embellishments</li>
<li>Egg Carton</li>
<li>Modge Podge or Polyeurothene</li>
</ul>
<h2>Step 1 &#8211; Decorate the Cookie Sheet</h2>
<p>First lay down some newspaper, and then have the kids paint a design on their cookie sheet. I used on old egg carton to separate my acrylic paints, and it worked great. After it dries, add some more stickers or embellishments around the border. I also had the kids add some big chunky letters spelling out their name. Upon completion add Polyurethane. Modge Podge is probably the more standard item. I just didn&#8217;t want to spend more money and already had the Polyurethane.</p>

<h2>Step 2 &#8211; Print Out Some Chores</h2>
<p>Here is a <a href="http://shared.confessionsofahomeschooler.com/momstuff/ChoreCards2x3.pdf">nice free selection of chore cards</a> at Confessions of a Homeschooler Mom. I printed them out at 50% of the actual size to accommodate the cookie trays. You may want to keep actual size depending on your preference.</p>
<p>Those got me started, but I also wanted to include a couple more. You are free to use the cards that I created and added for my kid&#8217;s chore charts.</p>

<h2>Step 3 &#8211; Laminate the Chore Cards</h2>
<p>Laminate the chore cards by taking strips of packaging tape and sealing the paper one section at a time. Then cut the chore cards out individually.</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-3570" alt="Chore Cards Laminated" src="/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/laminate.jpg" width="1200" height="900" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/laminate.jpg 1200w, /wp-content/uploads/2014/03/laminate-400x300.jpg 400w, /wp-content/uploads/2014/03/laminate-750x562.jpg 750w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1200px) 100vw, 1200px" /></p>
<h2>Step 4 &#8211; Add Magnets</h2>
<p>Lastly, add a magnet to the back of each chore card and voila your project is complete.</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-3576" alt="Chore Magnets" src="/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/magnets.jpg" width="756" height="629" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/magnets.jpg 756w, /wp-content/uploads/2014/03/magnets-400x332.jpg 400w, /wp-content/uploads/2014/03/magnets-750x624.jpg 750w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 756px) 100vw, 756px" /></p>
<p>I keep the chore magnets in a canister, but whatever you have that can contain them would work just fine. When it&#8217;s chore time they reach their hand in and are able to pick out a chore. Both my 3 and 4 year old can handle this. Each stage requires a different level of attention. My 3 year old is getting better but generally needs to be coaxed and assisted in her chores, or she gets side tracked. Whereas my 4 year old, pretty much can handle most of his chores independently. I just love this chore chart! I have tried various styles and this one has been my favorite.</p>
